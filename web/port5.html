Part 5: two fillers, a wobbling lighting table, and a cross fade through the palette

Block 5 of DOPE.EXE: the engine of parts 3 and 7 (PORT3.md, PORT7.md) a third time, on the picture part 4 leaves. part5.js is a subclass of part3.js; tools/addrmap.py and tools/ndiff.sh found what differs (listings with the numbers blanked out), and the differences are the study:

Two ways to fill a triangle

The four materials of the polygon table (cs:0 for the lookup picture, cs:8 for the lookup map) are not all phong. Where the map entry is 0 the polygon is filled by a plain texture mapper (raster3.js, texturePolygon): one attribute, the rotated normal's x,y, used as a position in a 256 x 256 texture, texture[(v << 8) | u]. Block 7 contains the same code and never takes it; here three of the four materials do.

Quirks that had to be kept exactly:

  • the inner loops count pixels in al: a run of 256 pixels or more draws its count modulo 256, and a run that happens to be 256 long draws 256;
  • the unclipped loop and the clipped loop patch different instructions, so the variables at their addresses differ (tpatchL...);
  • the divisions for the per row slopes use cwd (one byte, 99) and not cdq (66 99): the dividend is the sign extension of ax. The first version of the port took the instruction for a cdq, tracked eax's upper half and was wrong; the 16 bit disassembly prints both as cdq;
  • shld ebx, ecx, 10 instead of 8. Block 3's phong lookup gets the 7 bit x and y of the map from the top byte of each lane (shld 8). Here the author shifted by 10, which moves the low six bits of the y byte into the high byte of the index: bx = ((ecx >> 8 & 0x3f) << 10 | ecx >> 22) & 0x7f7f. The result is a lookup that is x scaled by 4, y mixed with x's high bits: an accident that makes the lighting look like it does. It showed up as a single pixel one colour off in frame 883 of 2,253.

The row table is rebuilt by the timer

cs:0x375b holds the 256 words the phong inner loop adds to the pixel's row (mov si, [cs:eax*2 + 0x375b]). In part 3 it is a constant. Here the timer interrupt rebuilds it every tick (0x3199): a running angle [0x1546] moves by [0x1548], its sine goes through dx = (sin >> 4) * 3 >> 4, and from dx a 16 entry ramp r[k] = ((k - 8) * dx) >> 8 is made; the table is every pair (r[i] << 8 | r[j]). The lit sphere breathes in and out.

The vertex stage differs

The tex coordinate of a vertex is read from the object (fs:[fs:0xa + 4v]) and not computed from the normal, and the rotated position is shifted by the object's offset again after the rotation (add eax, [0x14fa]: the object turns around its own centre and is also translated by it).

The screen is not cleared

The part begins on the picture part 4 left (web/data/handoff4.z). Its low nibbles become the background layer and the frame's first content (the objects use colours 16..255); the 16 first DAC colours are saved. At the end the high nibbles are filled with the next part's picture and the 16 x 16 palette is cross faded between two 16 colour palettes through two ramps (mixPalette of part 4, all 16 rows): fading the palette fades one picture into the other without touching a pixel. The fade is paced by the card's retrace, 64 steps, not by the timer.

Checked

tests/test_port3_run.mjs --block 5: the original from its first instruction, timer held back, 2,253 frames, after each one every variable, the lookup row table, the frame, the picture and the DAC are equal; then the cross fade at its first and last step. --poly checks polygon by polygon inside the frame (the port draws each triangle the original is about to draw and compares the frame and the variables when the original returns): that is how the shld 10 was found.
