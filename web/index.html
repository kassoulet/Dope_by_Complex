The original

DOPE.EXE (584,133 bytes) by Complex: code jmagic, graphics and music jugi, boxes pixelled by reward. It wants 550k of base memory, 600k of extended memory and a Gravis UltraSound with 512k: the music is a 28-channel ProTracker module mixed by the GUS hardware, not by the CPU. The readme admits the end scroll was never made.

Aspect ratio

The demo runs in VGA mode 13h: 320×200 pixels on a monitor that was 4:3. Pixels were therefore 1.2× taller than wide. The screenshots above are 320×200 and are shown at 4:3 here; the player does the same with CSS (aspect-ratio: 4/3) and nearest-neighbour scaling. The button toggles to the raw 16:10 shape if you want to see the difference.

How it plays here

Part 1 (the first 39 s, the "COMPLEX" logo) is transcoded: its picture and palette tables are read out of the unpacked block and the frames are written directly (pixel-exact, no emulator). Parts 2 to 7 (credits, "7800 PHONG polys", "FIRE clouds / FRACTAL scape", "ENV mapping", "SHADOW volume / material MORPH", "LIGHT sources") are the original machine code: the page unpacks the blocks out of DOPE.EXE and runs them in a 16/32-bit real-mode x86 interpreter written in JavaScript (web/js/vm/), inside a Web Worker, with a model of the VGA card behind it. Nothing of the effects is reimplemented, so the pictures are the demo's own.

  1. The loader. Each part is mapped at segment 0xf8b, 23-28 words are relocated (+0xf7b), a few parts get a far init call first, then main runs. The relocation lists are not in the file as far as I could find; they were measured by diffing a DOSBox-X memory snapshot of every part at its start against its block (tools/blocksnap.py, tools/relocdiff.py, data in web/data/parts.json).
  2. The clock. A part's whole animation hangs on its own INT 8 handler at the monitor's 70 Hz. The page raises that interrupt from the audio clock (70.086 per second), so the animation keeps the original's speed whatever the interpreter manages. Each part lasts a fixed number of ticks (measured by running it to its int 21h/4Ch: 8200, 3665, 7443, 4557, 4063, 2494), which makes the whole demo 7:53.
  3. Pause and seek. Pause stops the interrupts and the interpreter. Seek restarts the part at its start (on the screen the previous part left, three small assets, web/data/handoff*.z) and calls the interrupt fast, about 1500 instructions per tick, until the part's own counters have caught up; a minute into a part costs a second or two.
  4. Speed. The interpreter does about 10 million instructions a second in Chrome. A 1995 Pentium did several times that, so the parts draw fewer frames per second here (roughly 5 to 15) than they did then. The speed of the animation is right; the smoothness is not.

The process

  1. Look at the file. A DOS MZ header, then eight Rob Northen RNC (ProPack method 1) blocks found by scanning for RNC\x01. Block 0 (25,570 bytes unpacked) is a DPMI extender plus a GUS player: Oskari Tammelin's ProTracker for GUS, patched to upload samples into GUS RAM. Blocks 1..7 are the demo parts, loaded one at a time as overlays.
  2. Unpack. tools/rnc.py (and the identical web/js/rnc.js) decompress all eight blocks; the CRCs stored in each header match.
  3. Disassemble. With ndisasm. The first guess (32-bit protected mode) was wrong: the parts are 16-bit real-mode code that uses 32-bit registers, and part 1 reprograms VGA mode 13h into a planar layout.
  4. Get ground truth. tools/refcapture.py boots the real DOPE.EXE in DOSBox-X with GUS emulation and saves 320×200 frames, optionally tagged with the part's counter read out of guest memory. tools/blocksnap.py watches the INT 8 vector to snapshot every part at the moment it starts.
  5. Transcode part 1. Once the picture layout (the card's planes read back four pixels per byte, CRTC start/offset counted in plane bytes) and the timer were right, part 1 reduced to a window into the unpacked picture plus two palette ramps (web/js/part1.js): 11 of 15 reference frames are byte-identical, 4 differ by 8 pixels each.
  6. Run the rest instead of rewriting it. A small x86 interpreter and VGA model (web/js/vm/) first served as a measuring rig, then turned out to be the faithful way to play parts 2 to 7. The reversing was the loader, the entry points, the timer model, and what the card has to do (reads of A0000 as well as writes: block 5 repaints over the checker block 4 left on screen).
  7. Check the interpreter against a real x86 model. tools/diffcheck.py and diffcheck2.py replay the interpreter's trace in Unicorn (QEMU's CPU) instruction by instruction, comparing registers, flags and every store, feeding it the same port reads and interrupts. It found, one at a time: shld/shrd with an immediate count executed as byte operations (the texture mapper's coordinate packing, which is why the first discs looked like moiré), rotates clobbering ZF/SF/PF, imul never setting CF/OF, and string instructions stepping SI/DI as 32-bit registers (a rep movsd across 64 KB, which hung part 6).
  8. The music. web/js/mod.js is a 28-channel ProTracker replayer. Against libopenmpt the loudness envelope correlates at 0.994 over the first 20 s; over the whole tune the clocks drift by 0.3 s. Seeking skips ticks without mixing and is bit-identical to continuous playback (tests/test_transport.mjs).

Everything is in the repository: docs/ANALYSIS.md and docs/RUNTIME.md hold the notes and measurements.

What is real and what is not

piecestate
unpacking, module parsingexact (CRC-checked)
part 1 (first ~39 s)transcoded from the original data, pixel-exact on 11/15 reference frames
parts 2 to 7the original code, emulated. Not a port of the effects to JavaScript: the 1995 instructions run in a JS x86. Pictures pinned by tests/test_partvm.mjs; the interpreter agrees with Unicorn on the instruction streams checked (1.5 M instructions of the texture loop, 0.6 M of part 6's start)
exactnessnot pixel-for-pixel against DOSBox. The original is not deterministic in the counter (which credit band is up at counter 127 differed between two DOSBox runs), so there is no single frame to match; compared by eye, shapes, lighting and titles agree
speed5-15 frames per second instead of whatever a Pentium managed; animation speed is right
musicthe original module; own replayer, not bit-exact to the GUS

Checks in headless Chrome (tests/test_browser.mjs): audio produces samples, pause freezes clock, picture and sound (also inside an emulated part), seeks at 0:30, 2:00 and 6:00 land on the right part, the loop at the end, and ?vm=1.
